# Intakes

How we design intakes today.

## Compliant or Rigid: Pick One

- **An intake either flexes and gives on impact, or is rigid enough to stow back. Pick one and commit to it.**
  *Why:* the original 2026 off-season intake tried to be both and did neither well. *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*
- **Default to a compliant polycarb design** over full metal.
  *Why:* cheaper and easier to replace. Switching to polycarb arms with a split-motor design was a great fix. *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*
- **If the intake is rigid, sync both sides with a jackshaft.**
  *Why:* it would have kept the rigid version moving together. *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*

## Compliance

- **Use stacked 1/4" polycarbonate as a leaf spring** for flex.
  *Why:* it flexes very well. *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*

## Acquisition

- **Go for a big acquisition zone:** a cross bar with a 3" diameter roller worked well.
  *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*
- **Hold the intake motor with metal plates.**
  *Why:* it kept the pulleys and belts aligned. *([2026 Off-Season](../../seasons/2026/off-season.md))*

## Guidance

- Any arm that extends on a rack needs guides on both sides. See [Linear Motion](linear-motion.md#rack-and-pinion).
