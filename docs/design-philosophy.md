# Robot Design Philosophy

## Season Goals

TorBots sets two goals each season, in order:

- **Threshold goal:** qualify for the Southern California District Championship (DCMP).
- **Objective goal:** qualify for the FIRST Championship (Worlds).

The threshold goal is the minimum bar for a successful season. The objective goal is the stretch target — worth pushing for, but not at the cost of missing DCMP.

## Design for the Whole Team

A good robot design isn't just one that could theoretically score a lot of points. It's one **this team** can actually build, wire, program, and keep running all season.

That means designing around students and mentors together, not students alone. If any part of the team can't sustain a mechanism — not enough students, not enough experience, not enough mentor time — it's the wrong choice, no matter how good it looks on paper.

Design for the team we have *this year*, not the team we had two years ago or the team we wish we had.

## Student and Mentor Capacity Are Real Constraints

A mechanism only works if the students building it have the numbers and experience to pull it off, not just the potential to learn it eventually. One student who understands a mechanism, with no one else backing them up, is a risk — if they're out sick or graduate, that knowledge is gone. Aim for designs the whole subteam can own, not just its strongest member.

The same goes for mentors. **Even if students can build something, if the mentors backing them don't have the time or expertise to support it, it's not going to work.** Mentor support is a real constraint, same as budget, timeline, or shop access.

Neither side covers for the other. A strong student group with thin mentor support is just as risky as strong mentors trying to carry a thin student group.

## In Practice

Before committing to a mechanism, ask:

- Can our current students build and maintain this reliably, not just in theory?
- Does that knowledge live with one or two people, or is it spread across the subteam?
- Do the mentors involved have the time and expertise to support this through build and competition?
- If it breaks at competition, can we fix it, or does it depend on one person who might not be there?
- Is the upside toward Worlds worth risking DCMP?

When in doubt, pick the mechanism the team already understands well over a new one you'd be learning under competition pressure, unless you genuinely have the student depth and mentor support to take it on.
